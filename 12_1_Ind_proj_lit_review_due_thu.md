# Individual project: diversity of models

**Due:** Thursday 5 Nov 3:30 PM (before class)

**Grading criteria:** Complete the task and present in class.

**Percent of grade:** 5% of individual project grade (ca 1.5% of total grade)

The first task for your individual project is to spend a few hours researching process models relevant to your project. Find papers that describe process models for your ecological system or for the general questions you are interested in asking. Try to find at least 3 models of different types. If you are working in an area rich with models, you'll likely find a range of different types of models (e.g differential equations of various kinds, difference equations, individual based models, matrix models, stochastic/deterministic, etc) of various degrees of complexity.

On Thursday, we'll share notes about models and data. 

Preparation:

For **three** models you find, add a brief description to this slide deck, **one slide per model**.

* https://docs.google.com/presentation/d/1NcueuXjVKPQlFYep-7OjcgVYxB0m8mad5XRQ5nNHKto

A typical brief description will provide:

* The nugget of the model, e.g. algorithm or equations (a screen shot is sufficient).
* What ecological/evolutionary processes it includes.
* A plot of simulation results or fit to data, might also be relevant or useful.

If you find more than three models (likely), try to include a diversity in those you share.

Each person will speak to their slides for 5-10 minutes. This is low-stakes sharing what we find. The goal is to gain a quick overview of the diversity of models in our sphere. The goal is not to understand or explain each model in detail, so I'm not expecting you to spend a lot of time to understand each model or to demonstrate an understanding of the model beyond what type it is and a broad brush of how it attempts to model key processes.
